# Week 04 Reflection — Data & Deep Learning Foundations

## Reflection

Week 04 helped me understand how neural networks learn from data and how deep learning can be applied to engineering problems. I learned that data quality, network architecture and training methods influence model performance. I also understood that a more complex network does not always produce better results, and evaluating performance on unseen data is essential. Connecting these concepts to Physical Design helped me see how ML could support tasks such as timing-risk prediction and congestion estimation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Data Quality Matters:** A neural network learns from the examples provided, so accurate labels and representative data are essential.
- **Understanding Neural Networks:** Forward propagation, loss calculation, backpropagation and optimisation work together to train a model.
- **Model Complexity:** Adding layers and neurons can help learn complex patterns, but it may also lead to overfitting.
- **Evaluation and Generalisation:** Training performance alone is not enough; unseen data must also be used to assess reliability.
- **ML in Physical Design:** ML could help predict timing risk and support design optimisation, but final decisions must rely on validated engineering tools.

## Future Goals

- Practise neural-network experiments using TensorFlow Playground and compare different architectures.
- Learn how to identify overfitting and improve model generalisation.
- Develop a stronger understanding of precision, recall and other evaluation metrics.
- Explore ML applications in Physical Design, particularly timing prediction, congestion estimation and PPA optimisation.
- Learn how to validate ML predictions using real engineering requirements while maintaining reliable sign-off practices.

## Final Thought

This week strengthened my understanding of the relationship between data, neural networks and model performance. My main takeaway is that building a larger model is not necessarily the goal; building a reliable model that generalises well is more important. In the future, I want to apply these concepts to Physical Design problems and understand how ML can support engineers in making better-informed decisions.
