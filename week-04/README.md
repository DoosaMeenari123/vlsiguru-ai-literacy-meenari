## 1. Overview
This assignment explores how data is prepared for deep learning, how neural networks process information, how models learn from errors, and how their performance is evaluated.
The concepts are connected to semiconductor engineering, particularly chip inspection, defect detection, and Physical Design (PD). The objective is to understand how deep learning can help solve engineering problems and why data quality, suitable evaluation, and generalisation are important for reliable results.

## 2. Learning Objectives
- Understand image data preparation, labelling, and data quality.
- Compare rule-based programming, traditional Machine Learning (ML), and Deep Learning (DL).
- Explain neural-network layers, neurons, weights, and biases.
- Understand forward propagation, loss, backpropagation, and optimisation.
- Differentiate between training, inference, and generalisation.
- Observe how model complexity affects learning and performance.
- Evaluate a potential deep learning application in Physical Design.
- Identify the limitations and risks of using deep learning in engineering.

## 3. Missions
### Mission 1: Data Behind Deep Learning
Understand how raw data is collected, cleaned, labelled, and prepared for training. Explore how missing values, incorrect labels, noisy images, and unrepresentative datasets affect model performance.
### Mission 2: What Makes Deep Learning Different?
Compare rule-based systems, traditional ML, and DL. Understand how neural networks learn complex patterns from data instead of relying entirely on manually written rules or predefined features.
### Mission 3: Open the Neural Network
Explore the structure of a neural network, including the input layer, hidden layers, output layer, neurons, weights, and biases. Understand how information moves through the network to generate predictions.
### Mission 4: How Does a Neural Network Learn?
Understand the learning process through forward propagation, loss calculation, backpropagation, and optimisation. Learn how network parameters are adjusted to reduce prediction errors during training.
### Mission 5: Experiment and Observe
Investigate how model complexity influences learning. Understand underfitting, overfitting, and generalisation, and observe why a more complex network does not always perform better on unseen data.
### Mission 6: Final Challenge — Engineering Case Study
Explore a potential deep learning application in semiconductor engineering or Physical Design. Identify the engineering problem, required data, expected output, learning approach, evaluation method, and limitations.

### Mission 7: Sources and References
Collect reliable technical documentation, tutorials, and research resources that support the concepts discussed throughout the assignment.

### Mission 8: Final Reflection

Summarise the important concepts learned, the challenges encountered, and how deep learning knowledge can support future work in Physical Design.

## 7. Key Learnings

Deep learning allows a model to learn complex patterns from data through multiple layers of processing. However, its effectiveness depends on the quality of the data, the suitability of the network, and the way its performance is measured.

In semiconductor engineering, deep learning could help classify defects in inspection images, identify unusual patterns, or support Physical Design tasks such as congestion prediction. Such applications require relevant training data, appropriate evaluation, and engineering validation before being used in real workflows.

A complex model is not automatically a better model. A useful model should learn meaningful patterns, avoid overfitting, and perform reliably on data it has not seen during training.
