# Week 2 – Machine Learning Reflection
# 1. What I Learned
This week, I learned the basic concepts of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML), and how they are connected to Deep Learning, Generative AI, and Large Language Models (LLMs).
I learned about datasets, samples, features, labels, and different learning approaches, including supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, and self-supervised learning. I also understood how models are trained, how they make predictions, and how their performance is evaluated using different metrics.
I learned why data quality, model evaluation, and generalisation are important. I also explored how machine learning can be applied to semiconductor engineering, especially in VLSI Physical Design.

# 2. Most Important Insight
My most important insight is that machine learning is not the best solution for every problem. Before choosing ML, we should understand the problem, check the available data, and decide whether a simple rule-based approach is sufficient.
I also learned that a model performing well on training data does not guarantee good performance on new data. Therefore, proper testing, good-quality data, and continuous monitoring are important for building reliable ML systems.
Another important lesson is that incorrect predictions can have real-world consequences. We must understand a model's limitations and use its predictions carefully.

# 3. Connection to Physical Design
As a VLSI Physical Design learner, I understood how machine learning can support engineering tasks such as timing prediction, semiconductor defect detection, and Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) estimation.
For example, an ML model could learn from historical design data, such as cell count, congestion, utilisation, and timing results, to estimate whether a new design is likely to meet timing requirements.
Similarly, ML can help identify possible manufacturing defects or estimate power, performance, and area before completing the full design flow.
However, ML predictions should support engineering decisions rather than replace proper EDA tool analysis, verification, and final sign-off.

# 4. Challenges Faced
One challenge I faced was understanding the differences between AI, ML, Deep Learning, and Generative AI. I also needed to understand how the different learning approaches work and when each one should be used.
Another challenge was understanding model evaluation, generalisation, and why a model may fail when it receives new or different data. Learning about accuracy, precision, recall, and other evaluation methods also required careful understanding.
Connecting these concepts to VLSI Physical Design was another learning challenge. Working through examples such as timing prediction, defect detection, and PPA estimation helped me understand how ML concepts can be applied to practical engineering problems.
